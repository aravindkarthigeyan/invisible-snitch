The Invisible Snitch

Harry starts at the centre and runs at speed at most 1. The Snitch starts at distance 1 in a direction he cannot see and always flees straight away from him at speed v: S′ = v·(S − H)/|S − H|. Harry must commit to his whole path in advance and come within his vision radius ε of the Snitch, whichever direction it started in. Every gold dot below is one possible Snitch. The path is the one from the proof: once every dot has turned grey, Harry has won against all of them at once.

The path, in four phases

1 · Run through

Straight through the front

Harry runs straight past first contact and on for distance 1. Stopping at contact would leave a corner; running through leaves a smooth front with a small seen hole.

2 · Touch down

Meet the front at a tangent

He heads for the candidate at angle arccos(v + 1/t₁), chosen so the second straight run grazes the front instead of hitting it head-on.

3 · Walk

Walk the front, step ε

He repeatedly heads for the next unseen candidate. The front ahead stays flat at every scale, which is the heart of the proof.

4 · Close

Into the end left behind

Having gone all the way round, he walks into the end he left at touch-down. Because he left it tangentially, it has not curled into a spiral.

How the proof fits together

§6–7
Touch-down data

Two straight runs have exact formulas, so the front at touch-down is known exactly: its tilt shrinks at small scales both ahead of Harry and at the end left behind.

§3–4
End theorem

Walking the front in steps of ε, the gap between arc and chord for every pair of points stays bounded at every scale, right up to the last end, with constants independent of ε.

§5
Heading and departure

The end left behind at touch-down keeps its good shape while Harry walks away from it, then rejoins the main argument.

§8
Every direction seen by 13.1

Assembling the pieces: for every ε and v ≤ 5.8·10⁻³ all directions are seen within a time that does not depend on ε.

§2 · Cor.
Exact capture at ε = 0

Because the time bound is independent of ε, a limit of these paths catches every direction exactly.

How far the proven speed has come

Largest Snitch speed with a proof of exact capture, on a logarithmic scale.

The simulation uses the proof's path with a simple greedy walk: Harry always heads straight for the next unseen candidate in order around the front. The modified Snitch rule v·(S−H)/max(|S−H|, ε) is used for every candidate, as in the proof; it matches the real Snitch until the candidate is first seen. “Stop at contact” is the obvious start the proof avoids: Harry runs at the front and turns at first contact. The end he leaves behind curls into a slowly winding spiral, which is why the proof needed a different start.
