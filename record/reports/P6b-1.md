## 1. What order really is (P6b.1, P6b.2)

Let w = ∂_αθ be the α-derivative of the bearing along the front. (O) says w ≥ 0 on the remaining arc (plus strictness
and range [0, π]). Since w = |T| sin φ/ρ, the zero set of w is the set of *radial tangencies*: points whose tangent
line passes through Harry. Two facts decide everything:
 * ∂_t w = |T| sin(φ − β)/ρ² on a leg. At a radial tangency with the tangent pointing away from Harry (φ = 0) this is
   −|T| sin β/ρ² < 0: **w = 0 is not a barrier**, Harry simply crosses the tangent line. So order is not preserved by a
   maximum principle, for any v. (Small v does not help: v does not appear in this rate.)
 * ∂_α w = ±|T|²κ/ρ at a radial tangency. So a zero of w that appears *inside* an arc on which w ≥ 0 must be a double
   zero, i.e. sits at an inflection point.
Hence Proposition P6b.2: order is first lost exactly when Harry crosses the tangent line at an inflection point of the
remaining arc, or the tangent line at its far end (the target end is harmless: the tangent at the target obeys
φ' = (v/2ρ) sin 2φ and can never become radial). Equivalently: **(O) holds as long as the remaining arc is locally
strictly convex and its far-end tangent is not radial.** This is the precise form of "(a) → (b) → (c) proves (O) only
if strict order at leg ends is verified" (remark after D3.2): what has to be verified is a statement about curvature.

Consequence for the plan of L-6 §4: "prove (O) for small v" = "prove that the remaining arc has no inflection whose
tangent line meets Harry". Order is a *curvature-sign* condition seen from the near field, not an independent structure.

## 2. The end phase with ε > 0 (P6b.4, P6b.5)

Lemma A3.4 carries over to the ε-chase with the unseen set in place of the remaining candidates: the set
V(t) = cl U(t) ∪ {target} only shrinks, its points stay at distance ≥ ε/2 from Harry (so no catching issue, no proviso
(R)), and the perimeter P of conv({H} ∪ X(t)) obeys P(t') ≤ P(t) + G(γ)(t' − t) while the unseen set lies in a sector
of opening ≤ γ at Harry; P > 2ε while anything is unseen, and P ≤ 2Ψ. The proof reduces to the refereed finite lemma by
taking suprema over finite subsets containing the target. This gives Theorem P6b.5, the ε-analogue of Theorem A3.5,
with an ε-independent time. For γ = π/2 it works for all v < 1/(1+π) (c_γ = 1 − 1.5πv > 0 for v < 0.212).

Numerically (§4) B(t) ≤ π/2 already from the middle of the chase (static picture: B = half the remaining angle), so the
hull lemma can take over long before order is lost; (N) is needed only on the first half, and the tail hook (§3.2) is
then irrelevant for Ψ. But (S_γ) itself is a hypothesis on the dynamics: a sector at Harry is *not* forward invariant
(bearings move away from the heading at rate sin β/ρ, with no factor v, so one unseen point passed laterally at distance
slightly above ε opens the sector to π). Under (O) one has B = β(α_e), so on the ordered phase (S_γ) is a statement
about one bearing; after loss of order it is again a near-field statement.

## 3. The precise gap

### 3.1 What is missing
 (G1) **(N) up to a time t_1 at which the unseen set lies in a sector of opening ≤ γ_0** (any γ_0 < π − 2√(πv) will do),
 uniformly in ε, for v ≤ v_0.
 (G2) **(S_γ) after t_1** (B(t) ≤ γ for all later t).
Given (G1) and (G2), P6b.5 proves the round-6 theorem with v_0 < 1/(1+π) and T_0 ≤ 27.6 for v ≤ 0.1, γ = π/2.

### 3.2 Why (G1) is the near-field curvature problem
By P6b.3 (with κ_0 = 1) the front stays strictly convex at every direction with v e^{3vJ_1}J_2 < 1; e.g. at every
direction that Harry's path has not approached within r, as long as v e^{3vt/r} t/r² < 1 (r ≥ 2√(vt), vt ≤ 1/36
suffices). So inflections can only be created within distance ~√v of the path, and the dangerous ones are those
*ahead* of Harry at distances between ε and ~√v. There the exact law (Lemma A3.2) is
κ_t = (v/ρ)(1 − 3 sin²φ)κ − (v/ρ²) sin φ (3cos²φ − 1); for a point at arc distance s ahead of Harry on the arc
(φ ≈ ½·(mean curvature between) · s) the two terms cancel to leading order and leave
κ_t(s) ≈ (v/s)[κ(s) − (2/s²)∫_0^s uκ(u) du] (L-6 §2). This is neutral for constant curvature but has **no positivity
principle**: where κ(s) is below its weighted mean on [0, s], it decreases, at rate v/s, which is not integrable down
to s = ε unless κ has a modulus of continuity (a Hölder bound on κ at scale s gives an integrable rate
v[κ]_γ s^{γ−1}). I tried the obvious invariant cones (κ ≥ 0; κ monotone along the arc; κ ≥ weighted mean) and none is
preserved (for κ = a + b s^p the "κ ≥ mean" cone is preserved only for p ≥ 1). So (G1) needs exactly the scale-weighted
Hölder control of the curvature of the front ahead of Harry asked for in L-6 §2 (stream P6a's object); with it,
κ = 1 + O(v) ahead of Harry, hence (N), hence (O), hence K ≤ π. Without it the order route has no independent proof.
The (C, h) structure does not help either: on a leg (O) is "the image curve F(arc) has monotone polar angle about the
moving point (0, (1−v²)s)", which is implied by convexity of F(arc), and a translation preserves that exactly; but the
corner map F∘R_χ∘F^{−1} is only homogeneous of degree 1, with second derivatives ~v/ρ, and does not preserve convexity
of an arc that is nearly radial — the same marginal cancellation.

### 3.3 The tail hook, and why K ≤ K* via total curvature does not come for free
The front at t_0 is strictly convex (NUMERICAL, 40-digit evaluation of the closed form, `tip_curv.py`: κ > 0 for all
tested α, with κ ~ ρ^{2v/(1−v)} → 0 at the tip). During the *first leg* a concave piece (one inflection) forms at the
tail end α ≈ 2π, which at t_0 is the unseen point nearest to Harry on the side he leaves behind (this is A-3's hook:
J_2 of the tail is ~1/ρ_tail ≫ 1/v, so P6b.3 gives nothing there). It is present once ε is small enough at given v (v = 0.1, 0.2, 0.3: at ε = 0.01; v = 0.05: at ε = 0.003 but not at
0.01; v = 0.01: not at ε = 0.003; by D3-1 order never fails at v = 0.01 down to ε = 10⁻³), so, extrapolating (NUMERICAL), **no ε-uniform proof of (N) on the
whole chase should be expected**: (N) fails at the end for the tested v once ε is small. Numerically it is the only
inflection, and order is lost exactly when Harry crosses its tangent line, at which time B ≤ 0.46.
For the crude route "K ≤ 2 + TC, TC ≤ any constant": TC' = Σ ±2Ω over inflection points ± Ω at the two ends, with
Ω = (v/2ρ) sin 2φ (Prop. A3.3). With at most one inflection, far from Harry, and the far end far from Harry, the only
dangerous term is the target end (rate v/ρ* with ρ* ≤ ε on ~1/ε legs: each leg can turn the target's tangent by an
amount that is not small in ε), and whether these contributions are consumed (the turned piece is seen within the
next legs) or accumulate is again the near-field regularity question (HEURISTIC, as in A-3 §5). A bound on the *number of inflections* would suffice
(K ≤ TV(bearing) and every monotone piece of the bearing is produced at an inflection or an end), but inflection
creation has no comparison principle (Remark after A3.3), and P6b.3 only excludes it in the far field. So for the
total-curvature variant the missing statement is: **the number of inflection points of the remaining arc within
distance √v of Harry is bounded independently of ε.** Not proved.

### 3.4 What I would do next
 1. Take (N) as the single target lemma for the bulk: "for v ≤ v_0 the remaining arc, minus its last piece of α-length
    δ(v) next to 2π, has κ ≥ 1/2", proved through the Hölder scheme of L-6 §2; P6b.2 then converts it into K ≤ π with
    no further work, and P6b.5 finishes if B ≤ π/2 is known from mid-chase on.
 2. For (G2) use that under (O) B = β(α_e): prove β(α_e(t), t) ≤ π/2 once the arc is half consumed (a far-field
    statement for small v: the far end is at distance ≥ chord − O(vT) from Harry, positions are within vt of the static
    ones, so its direction from Harry is the static one up to O(vT/chord) — PROVABLE in principle by E1-type bounds; not
    done here), and treat the last piece (hook inside a sector ≤ 0.5 rad) by the hull lemma with a separate argument
    that nothing unseen is passed laterally.
 3. Alternatively remove the hook by design (it is created by leaving the tail side of the first-contact tip unseen at
    distance ~ε from Harry): e.g. sweep the tip neighbourhood on both sides first. Not analysed.
