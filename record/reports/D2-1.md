# Report D2-1: the exact-front window walk. v ≤ c/log(1/ε) proved; absolute v0 reduced to a front-length bound

Formulation unchanged: d = 1, speed ≤ 1, path fixed in advance, ε ∈ (0, 1) fixed, S' = v(S − H)/|S − H|.
**Not yet refereed.** The PROVED statements, with full proofs, are in `referee/D2-claims.md` (Claims 1–5, Theorem D2,
Corollary D2.1). This report repeats them in condensed form, adds the gap analysis (§3) and the numerics (§4).

## 0. Result in one paragraph
**Theorem D2 (PROVED).** For every ε ∈ (0, 1) and every v ≤ v_2(ε) := 1/(22 log(d/ε) + 28), Harry has a path,
fixed in advance, of speed ≤ 1, that sees every direction by time (1 + 4π)d/(1 − v) < 14.1 d.
This replaces D1's v ≍ (ε/log(1/ε))^{1/2} by v ≍ 1/log(1/ε) (e.g. ε = 10⁻⁶: v_2 = 3.0·10⁻³ against D1's 1.7·10⁻⁵;
ε = 10⁻¹²: 1.6·10⁻³ against 1.2·10⁻⁸). v_2(ε) > v_D(ε) for every ε ∈ (0, 1), and v_2 > ε/(1 + 2π) (Theorem A) for
ε ≲ 0.09. The time bound is uniform in ε. **The absolute-v0 goal is NOT reached.** Corollary D2.1 (PROVED) reduces it
to one estimate: an ε-uniform bound on the total length walked by the same construction (equivalently, Lipschitz bounds
on the walked fronts) for some fixed v. Numerically (§4) that length is ε-independent to 3 digits at v = 0.1, 0.2, 0.25.

## 1. The construction and the proof (condensed; full text in referee/D2-claims.md)

**Key idea (re-anchoring, made exact).** Harry knows his own past path, so he can compute every candidate's position
exactly. To make this usable for *all* directions, including ones already seen (which may later be caught, where the
true dynamics is singular), use the **virtual candidates**
  S̃_θ' = v F(S̃_θ − P),  F(x) := x/max(|x|, ε) = π_ε(x)/ε  (π_ε = projection onto the ε-disc).
F is globally Lipschitz and *monotone* (projections are firmly non-expansive), and F = unit on |x| ≥ ε. Hence (Claim 2):
virtual candidates exist for all time, they coincide with the true ones until the first time they come within ε of
Harry (so "ρ̃_θ(t) ≤ ε at some t" ⇒ "θ seen by t"), their mutual distances never decrease, and their stretch obeys
  limsup_{φ→θ} |S̃_φ(t) − S̃_θ(t)|/|φ − θ| ≤ exp(v J_θ(t)),  J_θ(t) := ∫_0^t ds / max(ε, ρ̃_θ(s)).

**Construction W.** ℓ := ε/v. Connector 1: run straight to S̃_0 until meeting it (time ≤ 1/(1 − v)). Then repeat:
*walk j* — freeze the virtual front Γ_j(φ) := S̃_φ(τ'_j) on [a_j, 2π] and walk along it by arclength for length ℓ
(or to φ = 2π, then stop); *connector j + 1* — run straight at S̃_{a_{j+1}} until meeting it (it starts within vℓ = ε
of Harry, the chase is along a line, duration ≤ ε/(1 − v)).

**Seeing is automatic (Claim 4).** Each φ ∈ [a_j, a_{j+1}] is at Harry's position at time s_φ = τ'_j + σ_j(φ)
*as it was at time τ'_j*, and has moved at most vℓ = ε since. So every direction passed is seen. No prediction error,
no bootstrap: the only question is whether the walk *terminates*, and when.

**Time (Claim 5).** Bootstrap on Λ-Lipschitz fronts. During the walk Harry is within ε of the virtual candidate whose
parameter θ*(s) he is currently at; by non-decreasing mutual distances,
  max(ε, ρ̃_φ(s)) ≥ max(ε, χ(φ, θ*(s))/2),  χ = chord on the unit circle.
So the time Harry spends with ρ̃_φ small is controlled by the *walked length* over a parameter arc of angular size
≲ 2y: ≤ (2πΛy + 2ε)/(1 − v) for scale y (walk on Λ-Lipschitz fronts + at most 2 + 2πΛy/ℓ connectors of duration
≤ ε/(1 − v)). The layer-cake formula gives J_φ ≤ J*(Λ) with
  (1 − v)J*(Λ) = 2 + 2 log(2/((1 − v)ε)) + 2πΛ log(1/ε) + 2 + 2πΛ
(first two terms: the initial straight run; the rest: the walk). If vJ*(Λ) ≤ log Λ the bootstrap closes: all walked
fronts are Λ-Lipschitz, at most 1 + 2πΛ/ℓ windows, stopping time ≤ (1 + 2πΛ)/(1 − v). With Λ = 2 the condition is
implied by v ≤ 1/(22 log(1/ε) + 28) (explicit check in the proof of Theorem D2: vJ*(2) ≤ 0.6867 < log 2).

*Remarks.* (i) Optimising Λ (Λ ≈ 3) improves the constant to v ≤ (0.0527 − o(1))/log(1/ε) as ε → 0; not worth the extra
text. (ii) The path has speed exactly 1 until it stops. (iii) Compared with D1, nothing is predicted: the window-start
anchor is the exact (virtual) front, so the error per window is exactly vℓ = ε, not vℓ + O(v² log). The ε^{1/2} barrier
of D1 disappears; what is left is the stretching of the front.

## 2. Why the method stops at v·log(1/ε) = O(1) (PROVED observation, method-specific)
The proof bounds the front stretch by exp(vJ_θ), i.e. it uses |d/dt log|∂_θS̃|| ≤ v/ρ (the true rate is v sin²φ/ρ, φ the
angle between the front tangent and the direction from Harry). For *any* path that sees θ at time t_θ:
ρ̃_θ(0) = 1, ρ̃_θ(t_θ) ≤ ε and |ρ̃_θ'| ≤ 1 + v, so J_θ(t_θ) ≥ ∫_ε^1 dr/((1 + v)r) = log(1/ε)/(1 + v). So this crude
bound is ≥ ε^{−v/(1+v)} for every direction, the front-length bound it yields is ≥ 2π ε^{−v/(1+v)}, and feeding that into the
scale-y occupation times makes J ≳ ε^{−v/(1+v)} log(1/ε): the bootstrap cannot close for fixed v as ε → 0. Hence no
refinement of the bookkeeping alone gives an absolute v0; geometric input on sin²φ is necessary. This is exactly the
near-field obstruction of A-3/A-4, now isolated in the cleanest setting available (all candidates seen by construction,
no switching, no proviso (R), no prediction error).

## 3. The precise gap for the absolute-v0 goal
**Corollary D2.1 (PROVED implication).** For any v < 1, ε ∈ (0, 1): if every walked front Γ_j is Λ-Lipschitz on
[a_j, a_{j+1}], then construction W stops by time (1 + 2πΛ)/(1 − v) and every direction is seen. More generally (same
proof), W stops iff its total walked length W_tot is finite, and then the stopping time is ≤ (1 + W_tot)/(1 − v)
(connectors cost ≤ v W_tot/(1 − v) since each follows a walk of length ℓ = ε/v).

**Gap G (CONJECTURE).** There are absolute v0 > 0 and W0 < ∞ such that for v ≤ v0 and all ε ∈ (0, 1), W_tot ≤ W0.
By Corollary D2.1 this gives the goal with time ≤ (1 + W0)/(1 − v). Numerics (§4): W_tot ≈ 7.9 (v = 0.1), 10.4
(v = 0.2), 12.1 (v = 0.25), independent of ε ∈ [10⁻³, 5·10⁻²] to 3 digits.

**Important numerical caveat for how to prove G.** The *uniform* Lipschitz version (sup stretch ≤ Λ on walked parts) is
probably FALSE for fixed v as ε → 0: the maximal walked stretch grows slowly, like ε^{−cv} with c ≈ 1 (v = 0.2: 3.56,
5.00, 6.23 at ε = 0.02, 0.005, 0.002, away from the end of the circle; at the left-behind tip θ ≈ 2π it is larger:
22.9 at ε = 0.002), while W_tot stays constant. So G must be proved for an *integrated* quantity. Heuristic route
(CONJECTURE, not proved): the true stretch rate is v sin²φ/ρ; for a point ahead of Harry at distance ρ along the front,
sin φ ≲ (turning of the front between Harry and the point) + ε/ρ. If the front ahead has scale-weighted regularity
"turning over an arc of extent ρ ≤ Kρ^{1−cv}" (the C^{1,1−cv}-type condition of A-4 §4, which is consistent with the kink
amplification ρ^{−μ}, μ = v/(1−v)), then ∫ v sin²φ/ρ ds ≲ v ∫ (ρ^{1−2cv} + ε²/ρ³) ds = O(v) and the stretch is
bounded except on sets whose contribution to W_tot is integrable (the left-behind tip contributes ∫_0 h^{−cv} dh < ∞).
Two concrete obstacles: (a) a curvature evolution law for the front *ahead* of a walker that sits within ε of it
(A-3's κ_t = −2v e/ρ³ + O(φ²) with e ≤ ε, plus the linear amplification term), with the window re-freezing every ℓ;
(b) the left-behind tip at θ ≈ 2π, approached head-on during connector 1, which is walked last with misalignment
φ ≈ π/2; its stretch is ≈ (1/ε)^{cv} pointwise and must be budgeted in W_tot, not in sup norm.

Reading: (i) T (hence W_tot = (1 − v)T − 1 up to O(v)) is constant in ε to 3 digits for v ≤ 0.25. (ii) max vJ grows like
v·log(1/ε) exactly as §2 predicts for the crude quantity, yet the true stretch and T do not: the crude rate bound is
the only loss. (iii) The maximal stretch grows slowly (≈ ε^{−v}); the largest values sit at the left-behind tip
θ ≈ 2π. (iv) At v = 0.3, ε = 0.01 and at v = 0.25, ε = 0.005 the walk did not terminate by t = 200: the stretch blows up
at the left-behind tip θ ≈ 2π (body stretch stays ≈ 7.7). v = 0.25 works at ε = 0.01 but fails at ε = 0.005, so the
numerical range of construction W *shrinks as ε decreases* near v ≈ 0.25 (not checked against discretisation; N = 8000).
This is consistent with §3(b): the head-on tip has stretch ≈ ε^{−cv}, and the walk can stall there. So the tip is a
real obstacle for W itself, not just for the proof; for v ≤ 0.2 no failure was seen down to ε = 10⁻³. A modified
start that avoids a head-on approach (or that walks the tip region first, from the outside) is the natural repair.

## 5. Status
* PROVED: Claims 1–5, Theorem D2 (v ≤ 1/(22 log(1/ε) + 28) ⇒ all seen by 14.1 d), Corollary D2.1 (reduction of the
  absolute-v0 goal to a bound on the total walked length of construction W), §2 lower bound on the crude stretch
  integral (J ≥ log(1/ε)/(1 + v)).
* NUMERICAL: construction W sees everything with ε-independent time for v ≤ 0.25 (ε down to 10⁻³); fails to stop by
  t = 200 at v = 0.3, ε = 0.01; maximal stretch grows ≈ ε^{−v} while total length is constant.
* CONJECTURE: Gap G (ε-uniform total walked length for v ≤ v0), and the heuristic scale-weighted route of §3.
* NOT ACHIEVED: an absolute v0.
