# L-6: lead's heuristic analysis for round 6 (UNVERIFIED — every formula here is a CONJECTURE/heuristic to be checked)

Goal of round 6: an ABSOLUTE v0 > 0 such that for every ε ∈ (0,1) and v ≤ v0 Harry sees every direction by a time T(v)
independent of ε. With Lemma A4.1 (paper, lem:A41) this gives EXACT capture (ε = 0) for v ≤ v0: goal 1 of the brief.

Starting point: Theorem D2 (paper/sec_D2.tex): virtual candidates S̃' = v F(S̃ − P), F(x) = x/max(|x|, ε); Harry walks
the frozen virtual front in windows of length ℓ = ε/v. Corollary D2.6: if the walked fronts are Λ-Lipschitz (or merely: the
total walked length is bounded) uniformly in ε, we are done. D2 proves it only for v·log(1/ε) ≲ 1/22 because it bounds the
stretch rate by v/ρ.

## 1. Where the logarithm should disappear
For ρ > ε the exact stretch rate of the front at a material point is
    (d/dt) log|∂_θ S̃| = v sin²φ / ρ,
φ = angle between the front's tangent at that point and the line to Harry. Length: Λ' = v K, K = ∫ sin²φ/ρ ds (Prop D6).
Harry stands (within O(ε)) ON the front. If the front ahead of him is flat in the sense
    (FLAT_γ)   sin φ(s) ≤ C (s/r0)^γ   for ε ≲ s ≤ r0   (s = distance from Harry along the front),
for ANY γ > 0, then the near part of K is ≤ C² ∫_0^{r0} s^{2γ−1} ds/r0^{2γ} = C²/(2γ), no logarithm, and the far part is
≤ (length)/r0. Offsets of Harry from the true front of size O(ε) (windows, connectors) change sin φ by O(ε/ρ), costing
v ∫ ε²/ρ³ dρ = O(v): harmless. Inside the ε-disc the virtual field is the linear dilation v x/ε: stretch e^{O(v)}, and
it does not distort shape. So the whole problem is (FLAT_γ), uniformly in ε. A bounded curvature κ of the front within
distance r0 ahead of Harry gives γ = 1 (sin φ ≈ κ s/2 on a circle through Harry).

## 2. Heuristic evolution laws (derive and verify!)
Field relative to Harry V = v x/|x|, DV = (v/ρ)(I − r̂ r̂ᵀ). For a material point of the front, with tangent angle ψ:
    ψ_t = ± v sin φ cos φ / ρ,     stretch rate = v sin²φ/ρ,
    κ_t = ∂_s(ψ_t) − κ·(stretch rate) = (v/ρ)(1 − 3 sin²φ) κ − (v/ρ²)(3cos²φ − 1) sin φ      (signs to be fixed),
using ∂_s φ = κ − sin φ/ρ, ∂_s ρ = cos φ. Near Harry (φ small, ρ ≈ s, sin φ(s) ≈ (1/s)∫_0^s u κ(u) du):
    κ_t(s) ≈ (v/s) [ κ(s) − (2/s²) ∫_0^s u κ(u) du ]  =  (v/s) (κ(s) − weighted mean of κ on [0, s]),
while s decreases at rate ≈ 1 − v. Consequences (all heuristic):
 (i) constant curvature is exactly neutral (the two terms cancel) — a circle through Harry is self-consistent;
 (ii) each term ALONE amplifies like (s0/s)^μ, μ = v/(1−v): this is the "kink amplification" of report A-4 and the reason
      sup-norm bounds give ε^{−O(v)} (observed: max stretch grows like ε^{−v} in D2-1 and D3-1 numerics);
 (iii) if κ is γ-Hölder along the front, |κ(s) − mean| ≤ [κ]_γ s^γ and the rate v[κ]_γ s^{γ−1} is INTEGRABLE down to s = 0:
      total change of κ during the whole approach ≤ v[κ]_γ s0^γ / (γ(1−v));
 (iv) the Hölder difference of two material points at separation δ is amplified by ≈ (s0/δ)^μ before they are at the same
      scale: Hölder exponent γ0 in the far field becomes γ0 − μ near Harry — a FINITE loss of regularity (compare §linear
      of the paper: "steering is a derivative of order 1/(1−v)"). For small v, μ ≪ γ0, so there is room.
 So the natural invariant is a SCALE-WEIGHTED Hölder norm of the curvature (or of the tangent angle) of the front ahead of
 Harry, e.g.  N = sup_{ε ≤ δ ≤ s ≤ r0} |κ(s+δ) − κ(s)| / (δ^γ (r0/s)^{μ'}) , propagated by the window induction of D2.
 Structure that should make the induction close: (a) the near-field law at a material point involves only points NEARER
 to Harry (triangular); (b) each material point is approached once; (c) while a point is at distance ≥ r0 the flow map is
 smooth with bounds depending only on r0, v, T; (d) the initial front (circle after Phase 1) is analytic away from the tip.

## 3. The tip (start and end of the sweep)
Phase 1 (head-on run at θ = 0) leaves a singular neighbourhood of the first-contact point on BOTH sides: stretch
~ |θ|^{−v...} (infinite at first contact when ε = 0). On the side Harry walks first it is consumed at once. On the other
side (θ slightly below 2π) it is left behind and met last; D2-1 numerics: stretch there 1.1·10⁵ at v = 0.25, ε = 0.005 and
the walk fails; for v ≤ 0.2 it succeeds. Heuristic: by scale invariance the geometry of the left-behind tip at scale ε is
a universal "inner profile" depending only on v (compute it exactly from the straight-run integrals C = ρ sin β/tan^v(β/2),
h = ρ(cos β + v)); outside a blob of size M ε it should be flat with power-law decay. A blob of size Mε seen with vision ε
is a problem with scale ratio M, where the D2/D3.1 arguments work with log M instead of log(1/ε) — so the endgame may be
closable by a LOCAL application of the D2 proof. Alternatively redesign the start/finish. Note the stretch singularity is
integrable in θ, so LENGTH is fine: use length/L¹-type bounds near the tip, never sup-norm.

## 4. Second route: order
Prop D3.2 (paper/sec_D3.tex): if the bearings of the remaining arc never fall out of order (O), then K ≤ π and every
v < 1/(1+π) wins uniformly in ε. Numerics (D3-1, RD3b): (O) holds until an end phase in which the remaining arc lies in a
sector ≤ 0.40 rad. Lemma A3.4 / Theorem A3.5 (hull perimeter, sector condition) handle an end phase inside a sector for
exact chases; an ε > 0 analogue plus a proof that (O) persists until the sector phase would also finish.

## 5. What success looks like
Theorem (target): ∃ v0 > 0 ∀ ε ∈ (0,1) ∀ v ≤ v0: an explicit path, fixed in advance, speed ≤ 1, sees every direction by
time T0·d (T0 absolute). Corollary (via lem:A41): exact capture for v ≤ v0. Intermediate results worth having: (FLAT_γ)
for the bulk conditional on the tip; the tip inner profile; a conditional theorem with ONE clean hypothesis checked
numerically; any improvement of v ≲ 1/log to v ≲ 1/log log.
