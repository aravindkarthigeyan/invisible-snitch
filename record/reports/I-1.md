## Result in one paragraph
I got all the primary sources. The creator-uploaded English captions, the description, both PDFs (as PDF and as text) and the public comments, including the author's replies, are in `refs/`. The formulation matches BRIEF.md in every material respect. The author's comments add three clarifications:
- Harry knows d only at t = 0.
- Harry knows both speeds.
- The arena is the infinite plane.

The author offers no prize and makes no claim beyond "I don't know" plus a stated belief that every v > 1 works, even with ε = 0. Both of the author's Phase-2 systems are singular at their own initial time, a point the PDFs miss (Section 1.4). No published work solves or bounds our problem. Two classical problems are close relatives:
- **Trawler (smuggler-in-fog) problem.** It has the same information structure. Capture holds for every speed ratio, because of linear front stretching plus a logarithmic divergence. This is exactly the mechanism behind the author's sufficient condition, and it is a provable model case for it.
- **Bressan's fire-blocking problem.** It is a front-versus-agent speed-threshold problem, open between Λ ≈ 1.626 and 2. Its lower-bound techniques (isoperimetric area bounds, a "last brick" argument for Jordan barriers) are the closest existing impossibility technology.

Nothing found implies capture or no-capture for any v in our problem.

### 1.2 Exact formulation in the video (transcript 01:28–02:44, paraphrase with key words quoted)

Author's proposed route (both PDFs). Phase 1: H = (vt, 0) until T1 = (d − ε)/(v − 1). Phase 2:
- **ε > 0 ansatz:** H(t) = S(θ(t), t) − ε ∂_tS(θ(t), t), with θ' = (√(v² − ε²|∂²_tS|²) − 1)/⟨∂_αS, ∂_tS⟩.

Sufficient condition (conjecture): |∂_αS(θ(t), t)| ≤ C(1 + t). This gives θ ≥ ((v − 1)/C) ln(1 + t), so θ reaches 2π in finite time. The ε = 0 PDF requires C to be "independent of time and of the angle along the pursuit trajectory".

Supporting toy model (ε = 0 PDF, PROVED there; I checked it): the circular front has a fixed centre. Then s(t; α) = Ce^{iα} + i t e^{iα/2}, so |∂_αs| ≤ C + t/2. The PDF itself says this does not prove the real case.

### 1.4 Two observations on the author's ansatz (derived here)

**(a) The ε = 0 system (6) starts at a singular point.** Status: PROVED, a short derivation from our Prop. 4 and Lemma 1.

With ε = 0, Phase 1 ends exactly at the contact time t0 = d/(1 − v) (ours). Each candidate keeps C = d g(α) during the straight run, and g(α) ~ 2^v α^{1−v} as α → 0. By Prop. 4, the bearing β(α, t0) → θ* with g(θ*) > 0. Hence

  ρ(α, t0) = d g(α)/g(β) ~ d 2^v α^{1−v}/g(θ*).

Because ρ(α, t0) ≤ ∫_0^α |∂_αS(·, t0)|, no bound |∂_αS| ≤ M can hold near α = 0, since α^{1−v} ≫ α. So |∂_αS(0⁺, T1)| = ∞. The author's θ'(0) = (v − 1)/|∂_αS| = 0, and condition (6) fails at t = 0 for every finite C. The front also has a corner at H: it arrives along two rays at bearings ±θ*.

In the author's normalisation the exponent is 1 − 1/v_author. Any proof along the author's line must therefore start Phase 2 from a regularised configuration, or use a growth bound of the form C(1 + t) only for t ≥ t1 > 0.

**(b) The ε > 0 system (3) has a vanishing denominator at t = 0.** Status: PROVED, by symmetry, assuming the front is smooth at T1. That is true for ε > 0 because T1 is before contact.

By the reflection symmetry S(−α) = conj S(α), at α = 0:
- ∂_αS(0, T1) is vertical;
- ∂_tS(0, T1) = (1, 0).

So ⟨∂_αS, ∂_tS⟩ = 0 at the start of Phase 2, while the numerator → v − 1 > 0. Thus θ'(0⁺) = +∞ formally. The author's code avoids this, because it advances θ by bisection on |ΔH| < v dt rather than integrating (3).

A related caveat on the author's simulator: it puts H exactly ε from a linearly *interpolated* point between two neighbouring candidates. It never checks that the actual candidates came within ε. "θ reaches 2π" in the code is therefore not a certificate that every candidate was seen.

## 2. Literature, ranked by relevance

Legend: [V] = checked against the source text, or at least the full abstract or page. [U] = from memory or secondary citation only, not checked.

**1. The trawler / smuggler-in-fog problem.** [V: MathWorld page; spiral fact derived here]
Citations: R. P. Agnew, *Differential Equations*, McGraw-Hill 1942, p. 303; L. R. Ford, *Differential Equations*, 1955, p. 32; C. S. Ogilvy, *Excursions in Mathematics*, Dover 1994, pp. 84, 148 (as cited by MathWorld "Trawler Problem"); P. J. Nahin, *Chases and Escapes*, Princeton 2007 [U that it covers this].

Result: a slow boat leaves a known point at known speed in an unknown fixed direction, and the fast pursuer is blind. The pursuer goes to a radius where the target could be, then follows a logarithmic spiral about the datum that keeps pace radially. It meets every direction within one turn.

In our normalisation (target speed v, pursuer speed 1) this is PROVED and elementary. Take r = vt and r θ' = √(1 − v²). Then θ = (√(1 − v²)/v) ln(t/t0), and a full turn finishes at t0·exp(2πv/√(1 − v²)). This is finite for **every** v < 1.

Bearing on our problem:
- The information structure is the same as ours: open-loop, known speeds, unknown angle.
- The capture mechanism is exactly the author's. The front (the circle of radius vt) has |∂_φS| = vt, which grows linearly, and the angle grows logarithmically. The trawler problem is the model case where condition (6) holds with equality.
- Difference: the target's law does not depend on the pursuer, and the pursuer crosses the front with tangential speed √(1 − v²) instead of creeping along it at 1 − v. In our problem the front is pushed by Harry; that is the whole difficulty, and our notes show a fixed log spiral catches only countably many directions.
- Technique to try: a comparison or sub-solution argument showing that the Snitch front near the caught point stretches no faster than some trawler front.
- Weak NUMERICAL aside: ln(sweep time) from BRIEF.md §3 against 2πv/√(1 − v²) has slope about 0.59–0.62 over v = 0.2–0.7. Six points cannot distinguish that from a power law in 1/(1 − v) (log–log slope 2.8–3.0).

**2. Bressan's fire-confinement (dynamic blocking) problem.** [V: Bressan–Talamini full text; Bianchini–Zizza abstract; others as cited there]
Citations:
- A. Bressan, "Differential inclusions and the control of forest fires", *J. Differential Equations* 243 (2007) 179–207.
- A. Bressan, M. Burago, A. Friend, J. Jou, "Blocking strategies for a fire control problem", *Anal. Appl.* 6 (2008) 229–246.
- S. Bianchini, M. Zizza, "Existence of spiral strategies for blocking fire spreading", arXiv:2508.05324 (2025).
- J. Schmidt, "A lower bound on the blob", arXiv:2609.13313 (2026).
- **A. Bressan, L. Talamini, "On the minimum speed for a fire blocking problem", arXiv:2610.36919 (27 Oct 2026).**

Result: fire spreads at unit speed and a wall is built at speed λ. λ > 2 suffices (Bressan 2007). The fire cannot be blocked if λ ≤ Λ = 1.62621…, by an isoperimetric bound plus a weighted double-bubble argument comparing two times (Bressan–Talamini, improving 3/2 from Schmidt). A Jordan-curve barrier needs λ > 2, by a "last brick" argument: the fire reaches the last point within half the barrier length. Spiral barriers work iff λ > 2.6144… (Bianchini–Zizza; parts are computer-assisted). The general conjecture "λ > 2 is necessary" is open; a prize was offered [U on the amount].

Bearing:
- This is the closest *threshold* problem with a spreading front and one speed-limited agent. It shows such thresholds can sit strictly inside the trivially admissible range.
- The lower-bound tools are area or isoperimetric bounds. Our notes already say area is not an obstruction for us, because our uncertainty set is a curve. The transferable idea is the "last caught point" argument: the last candidate Harry catches must have stayed uncaught while Harry traversed everything else. That could give a time lower bound, though probably not impossibility.
- It implies nothing directly for any v. It is the best template for stream G.

**3. Koopman's "flaming datum" search.** [U, except via search snippets]
Citations: B. O. Koopman, *Search and Screening: General Principles with Historical Applications*, Pergamon 1980; R. Hohzaki, A. Washburn, "The diesel submarine flaming datum problem", *Military Operations Research* 6(4) (2001) [U on details].

Result: a submarine flees a known datum in an unknown fixed direction at a known (maximal) speed. Koopman argues for spiral search; Hohzaki–Washburn treat it as a game with detection probability.

Bearing: this is the probabilistic or sweep-width version of item 1. If the target's speed is only bounded, the uncertainty set is a 2-D disc of radius ~ ut, and a searcher with fixed sweep width 2ε eventually cannot keep up. That is an area obstruction. Our uncertainty set is a 1-D curve, so the obstruction does not apply. Useful only as contrast.

**4. Linear search for an escaping target (1-D analogue).** [V]
Citations: J. Coleman, D. Ivanov, E. Kranakis, D. Krizanc, O. Morales-Ponce, "Linear search for an escaping target with unknown speed", arXiv:2404.14300 (IWOCA 2024). Earlier: Coleman–Kranakis–Krizanc–Morales-Ponce, OPODIS 2022 (LIPIcs 253, 12:1–12:19); S. Alpern, S. Gal, *The Theory of Search Games and Rendezvous*, Kluwer 2003, p. 134, eq. 8.25 [V only as cited].

Result: on a line, the target moves away from the origin at speed v < 1 from distance d in an unknown direction.
- With d and v known, the optimal competitive ratio is 1 + 2/(1 − v).
- With d unknown and v known, Alpern–Gal give 1 + 8(1 + v)/(1 − v)².
- With v unknown, the ratio is u^{4 − o(1)}, where u = 1/(1 − v).

Bearing:
- In 1-D the Snitch rule coincides with "away from the origin" once Harry is between the candidates. So the 1-D Snitch problem is solved for all v < 1, in time d(3 − v)/(1 − v)². PROVED, elementary.
- These papers use our exact device: they "imagine the targets from all problem instances as existing simultaneously", i.e. the wavefront.
- They confirm the difficulty is purely 2-D: the front stretches and Harry pushes it.

**5. Escape paths and spiral search against an unknown rotation.** [V for Langetepe–Kübel via ar5iv; others U]
Citations: R. Bellman, "Minimization problem", *Bull. AMS* 62 (1956) 270 ("lost in a forest"); S. R. Finch, J. E. Wetzel, "Lost in a forest", *Amer. Math. Monthly* 111 (2004) 645–654; E. Langetepe, D. Kübel, "Optimal online escape path against a certificate", arXiv:1604.05972; arXiv:2609.24454 (2026), "The logarithmic spiral is optimal for shoreline search: a computer-assisted proof" [abstract only].

Result: a single path fixed in advance must succeed against an unknown rotation or position, and logarithmic spirals are near-optimal or optimal.

Bearing: the same "path fixed in advance, unknown angle" quantifier structure. The adversarial-rotation lower-bound techniques might help prove a capture-*time* lower bound (goal 4). It says nothing about a threshold.

**6. Search games with a mobile, smart, invisible hider.** [U]
Citations: R. Isaacs, *Differential Games*, Wiley 1965 (princess and monster; homicidal chauffeur); S. Gal, "Search games with mobile and immobile hider", *SIAM J. Control Optim.* 17 (1979) 99–122; S. Gal, *Search Games*, Academic Press 1980; S. P. Lalley, H. E. Robbins, "Asymptotically minimax stochastic search strategies in the plane", *PNAS* 84 (1987) [author attribution and venue U].

Result: these are bounded domains with randomised strategies and smart evaders; the answers are in terms of expected capture time.

Bearing: essentially none. Our evader is deterministic and reactive. The one useful contrast is the Isaacs fact that running "directly away" is the optimal simple-motion evasion against a *visible* pursuer [U]. The Snitch plays the visible-game optimal policy while blind, which is why the problem is a pure open-loop control problem.

**7. Lion and man.** [U]
Citations: J. E. Littlewood, *Littlewood's Miscellany* (Besicovitch's solution); B. Bollobás, I. Leader, M. Walters, "Lion and man — can both win?", *Israel J. Math.* 189 (2012) 267–286.

Bearing: the pursuer sees the evader, so this is not relevant. The author cites "man and lion, Apollonius, dog and duck" only to say the prey is visible there.

**8. Zero-visibility cops and robbers, and blindfolded pursuit on graphs.** [abstracts only]
Citations: D. Dereniowski, D. Dyer, R. Tifenbach, B. Yang, "Zero-visibility cops and robber and the pathwidth of a graph", *J. Comb. Optim.* 29 (2015) [U]; "Capturing an invisible robber using separators", arXiv:2509.05024; "Blindfolded pursuit with delays of your choice", arXiv:2608.27347.

Bearing: these are discrete versions with a smart robber. None.

**9. Reframing as ensemble / open-loop control.** [abstracts only]
Citations: J.-S. Li, N. Khaneja, "Ensemble control of Bloch equations", *IEEE TAC* 54 (2009) 528–536 [U]; "Certified set convergence for piecewise affine systems", arXiv:2604.00286 (one open-loop input must steer every possible initial state).

Bearing: our problem is an ensemble *hitting* problem. One control H(·) is shared by a 1-parameter family of ODEs, and each member must hit the moving point H at some time; simultaneous arrival is not required. The existing ensemble theory targets simultaneous steering, so I know of no applicable result. The framing does justify our compactness lemma as the natural tool.

**10. Shepherding.** [U]
Citation: D. Strömbom et al., "Solving the shepherding problem", *J. R. Soc. Interface* 11 (2014) 20140719.

Bearing: the agents flee directly from the dog, which is our velocity law, but their positions are observed. Not applicable.
