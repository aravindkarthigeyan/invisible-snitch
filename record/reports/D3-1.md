# Report D3-1 (round 5, stream D3): finite chases with ε > 0

**Status.** The goal (an absolute v₀, independent of ε) is **not reached**. Proved instead:

* **Theorem D3.1 (PROVED).** For 0 < ε ≤ 0.1 and v ≤ v_W(ε) := 0.115/(log(1/ε) + 7.5), Harry sees every direction
  by time 22.8 along an explicit path of speed exactly 1. So the admissible speed decays only like **1/log(1/ε)**,
  instead of (ε/log(1/ε))^{1/2} (Theorem D1) or ε (Theorem A). Examples: v_W(10⁻²) = 9.5·10⁻³ (Theorem A: 1.4·10⁻³),
  v_W(10⁻⁶) = 5.4·10⁻³ (D1: 1.7·10⁻⁵), v_W(10⁻¹²) = 3.3·10⁻³ (D1: 1.2·10⁻⁸).
* **Proposition D3.2 (PROVED, (a)–(c); (d) a conditional reduction).** For a modified leg-greedy ε-chase (LG0) the
  bearing order (O) holds at the start, can only be lost through a pairwise reversal that is visible at the end of a
  leg, and is kept at switches. While (O) holds, K ≤ π, so Ψ decreases at rate ≥ 1 − v(1+π). If (O) never fails,
  every v < 1/(1+π) ≈ 0.2415 wins, uniformly in ε.
* **NUMERICAL.** (O) does fail, but only in an end phase. For v ≤ 0.05 and ε ≥ 0.01 it never fails; otherwise the first
  failure comes late (at 65–99.5% of the chase for v ≤ 0.4) and the remaining arc then fills a sector of opening ≤ 0.46 rad. Along
  LG0, sup K ≈ 2.0–2.2 for every tested (v, ε), against 3.4 for the LG chase of D-1.

## 1. Theorem D3.1: the stretch of the exact front is controlled by a logarithm (PROVED)

### 1.1 Idea

Theorem D1 walks along the *first-order prediction* Q_θ(t) of the front, built from the initial circle. Its error
is absolute, of size v²·(time)·log, and that error is the source of the (ε/log)^{1/2} barrier. Here Harry instead
walks along the **exact** current front Γ_k(θ) = S_θ(τ_k), which he can compute from his own path. He walks for a time
of at most about ε/v, so every walked candidate moves by at most a fraction of ε before he passes its recorded
position, and there is no prediction error at all. What has to be controlled is the length of the exact front, that
is, the stretch |∂_θS|. Two facts make this possible:

1. **Stretch identity** (Prop. 3.3(2) of the paper): ∂_t log|∂_θS| = (v/ρ) sin²φ ∈ [0, v/ρ]. Hence
   1 ≤ |∂_θS_θ(t)| ≤ exp(v∫_0^t ds/ρ_θ). The lower bound 1 comes for free and makes every Γ_k a regular curve.
2. **Lemma E1** (distances between candidates never decrease) turns Harry's position into a bound on all distances.
   While walking, Harry stands at H(s) = S_{θ'}(τ_k), so for every other candidate
   ρ_θ(s) ≥ |e^{iθ} − e^{iθ'}| − v(s − τ_k). So Harry can be close to θ only while he walks θ' ≈ θ, which happens once.
   Hence the time spent at distance < r from θ is O(r + ε). Since ρ ≳ ε for every candidate not yet walked, the layer
   cake gives ∫ds/ρ ≤ A log(1/ε) + O(1). This is the step that needs **v log(1/ε) ≲ 1**.

Two technical devices close the induction:

* **Skips.** At the start of a window the candidates just ahead of Harry have been close to him, possibly very close,
  so their stretch is not controlled. They are not walked. Each window starts η = 0.29ε beyond the previous stop, and the
  skipped candidates are shown to be within ε of Harry already, using the stretch bound from the *previous* window time.
* **Phase 1** is the straight run of Theorem D1, which sees the tip |θ| ≤ δ₁ = (0.3ε)^{1/(1−v)} (Lemma D3, re-checked
  for v ≤ 1/2). On this run the explicit integrals give ρ ≥ 0.29ε for every other candidate, and a monotone-ordinate
  argument covers the first connector.

### 1.2 Statement and proof

**Theorem D3.1 (PROVED).** Let 0 < ε ≤ 0.1 and v ≤ 0.115/(log(1/ε) + 7.5). Strategy W (§1.1 of `referee/D3-claims.md`;
parameters λ = 0.1, κ = 0.29, M = 3, walk length ℓ = λε/v, skip η = κε) has speed exactly 1, and every direction is
seen by time T_* ≤ 22.8.

The proof (Lemmas W1–W6 and the induction (H_k)) is given in full in `referee/D3-claims.md`, Claim 1. Its structure:

* (H_k): for every not-yet-walked θ ∈ [b_k, 2π − δ₁] we have ρ_θ ≥ ρ_* on [0, τ_k] and ∫_0^{τ_k} ds/ρ_θ ≤ J_*.
  With vJ_* ≤ log M this gives 1 ≤ |∂Γ_k| ≤ M = 3.
* Lower bounds on ρ (each uses Lemma E1 at a window start τ_j):
  * walks: ρ ≥ chord(min(η, 2δ₁)) − (λ+v)ε ≥ 0.175ε;
  * connectors: ρ ≥ chord(ℓ/M) − (1+v)ε, which is large because ℓ/M = λε/(vM) ≫ ε;
  * connector 1: lateral bound sin δ₁ − vε;
  * phase 1: ρ ≥ g(δ₁)/g_max ≈ 0.3ε.
* Time near θ: m(r) ≤ A r + B with
  * A = 2/(1−v) + (2π/3)M(1 + v/λ), where the term 2/(1−v) comes from phase 1 (Lemma W2: a straight run spends time
    ≤ 2r/(1−v) within r of any candidate), and the walks give (2π/3)M·r;
  * B = O(ε).
  The layer cake then gives J_* = A log(R/ρ_*) + B/ρ_* + T_*/R.
* Seeing:
  * walked candidates have moved at most v(c_k + ℓ) ≤ (λ + v)ε from their recorded position;
  * skipped candidates are within (λ + v + Mκ)ε ≤ ε of Harry at the next window start;
  * the tip is seen in phase 1.
* Constants (Lemma W6, `code/D3/constants.py`): r_* = ρ_*/ε ≥ 0.17495, A ≤ 9.2562, B ≤ 4.6792ε, T_* ≤ 22.7923.
  These give c = log 3/9.2562 = 0.11869 ≥ 0.115 and c' = 7.3671 ≤ 7.5. A brute-force check of all exact conditions on
  2000 points (ε from 10⁻¹ to 10⁻³⁰, v up to v_W) passes.

## 2. The precise gap

### 2.1 For strategy W

The only ε-dependence is J_* ~ A log(1/ε), which comes from ∫ds/ρ over a near pass (one unit per dyadic scale of ρ
between ε and 1). The true rate is (v/ρ) sin²φ, where φ is the angle between the front tangent ∂_θS and the direction
from Harry. When Harry walks along the front, the candidate he passes lies nearly along the front tangent, and
heuristically sin φ ≲ κ_front·ρ + ε/ρ. That would make every pass contribute O(v) instead of O(v log(1/ε)), and
v₀ would be absolute. **Missing:** a bound on the curvature κ_front of the exact front ahead of Harry that is uniform in
ε. The curvature is forced at rate ~ v/ρ² near Harry, and kink amplification (A-4) shows that it can grow like
(ρ₀/ρ)^{v/(1−v)}. That is again ε^{−O(v)}, harmless when v log(1/ε) = O(1) but not uniformly. This is the
near-field obstruction of rounds 3–4 in a scale-weighted form.

### 2.2 For the ordered route (Proposition D3.2)

(O) fails numerically in an end phase (§3.1). Two things are missing:

1. A proof that (O) holds up to an explicit end phase. Through Proposition D3.2(b) it suffices to check order at the
   end of each leg.
2. An end-phase argument after the first failure t_f.

Numerically, at t_f the remaining arc subtends at most 0.46 rad, and K ≤ 0.09. The natural tool is the hull perimeter
(Lemma A3.4), which needs a sector condition of opening γ with 1 + cos γ > v(π + γ). This is the exact analogue of
Theorems A2.3/A3.5, and like them it is **not proved**.

**Referee RD's proviso (R)** is not needed while (O) holds: under (O) only the target has bearing 0, so no arc point is
caught (Proposition D3.2(d)). It remains an issue only after t_f.

## 4. Routes considered and dropped

* **Route (i), stretch on [0, catch time] for the exact discrete chase.** The chase of N candidates catches each one
  exactly, and |∂_αS| = ∞ at a caught point. Pointwise stretch is useless near catches; only arc length is finite.
  This is why strategy W never walks candidates that have been near Harry (the skips) and never needs a catch.
* **Route (ii), a priori bootstrap of K ≤ π + O(v log(1/ε)).** It is replaced by the exact statement that K ≤ π while
  (O) holds (Proposition D3.2). A bootstrap of (O) itself was not found. The obstruction is the end phase, where the
  numerics show that (O) really fails.
* **Hull perimeter from the start.** At t₀ the opening is B_H = 2 arccos v ≈ π − 2v, but the condition
  1 + cos B_H > v(π + B_H) needs π − B_H ≳ 2(πv)^{1/2}. So the hull potential grows at first and is useful only in the
  end phase.
