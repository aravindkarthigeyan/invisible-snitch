## 0. Summary

* **The start is harmless (PROVED, Claim 1).** If Phase 1 is stopped *exactly* at first contact t_0 = 1/(1−v), the
  front is given by closed formulas. Everything in the singular blob (|α| ≤ α_s, g(α_s) = ε g_max) has already been
  seen; the two unseen arms start at distance ρ_s ∈ (ε, 1.4ε] from Harry and are *radially flat at every scale ≥ ε*:
  tan φ ≤ g(θ*) tan^{1+v}(α/2)/((1−v)(cos α − v)) ≲ ρ^{(1+v)/(1−v)}, with ε-independent constants
  (tan φ ≤ 0.77ρ for v ≤ 0.1, α ≤ 1/2). The only singularity is in the parametrisation: |∂_α S| ≍ α^{-v}
  (limit constant (1−v)2^v/g(θ*)). There is no "inner blob of radius Mε" to cross: M = 1.4.
* **The left-behind arm is unstable at a universal rate (PROVED in the straight-departure model, Claim 4).** An arm that
  Harry leaves along a line at wedge angle ω is rotated towards the perpendicular: |tan φ| ∼ c (s/r)^{v/(1+v)}, exactly
  computable; for the tip (ω = 2 arcsin v) |tan φ| ≤ 2v (s/r)^{v/(1+v)}/(1−4v²). A hook forms at scale ratio
  λ_c(v) ≈ (2v)^{-(1+v)/v}: 2.4·10²¹, 1.0·10⁸, 1.8·10⁴, 361, 42, 12 for v = 0.05, 0.1, 0.15, 0.2, 0.25, 0.3. The sup of
  the stretch of the left arm (relative to arclength at t_0) is therefore NOT ε-uniform for any v > 0; its length is.

  resolves the tip, both W and the cut version W_cut terminate for all tested v ≤ 0.4, ε ≥ 5·10⁻⁴, with stopping times
  ε-independent to 3–4 digits. So there is no evidence that the hook stops the walk.
* **One clean hypothesis (PROVED reduction, Claims 6–7).** For W_cut, if the growth rate of the remaining front satisfies
  K(t) ≤ κ with vκ < 1 − v, every direction is seen by T ≤ t_0 + 1.4ε/(1−v) + (2π/(1−v) + 1.4vκε/(1−v))/(1 − v − vκ).
  A sufficient geometric form: (bearing variation of the remaining front outside the ε-disc) + (its length inside)/ε ≤ κ.

## 5. What is not proved, and why the crude method cannot do it

* **(E) in the endgame.** When Harry returns, the left arm near its tip has been rotated and stretched as in §2
  (and more by the non-straight part of the path). To bound its length growth while he walks it one needs sin²φ, i.e. the
  same flatness analysis as in the bulk, started from a hooked arc. The local lemma (Claim 5) cannot replace it: any
  bound through J = ∫dt/max(ε, ρ) costs log(R/ε) for an arc of length R, and by §2 the arc that carries the hook has
  length ≫ ε (it lives at all scales ε ≤ r ≤ s/λ_c). The route I would take: prove (K_κ)-type bounds for a
  *scale-invariant* class of arcs — tangent angle Lipschitz in log(distance from the tip) with small constant (the model
  hook has |dψ/d log r| ≲ v/2) — which contains the hook and the Claim-1 arms, and is the natural class for P6a's weighted
  Hölder norm. CONJECTURE: (K_κ) holds for W_cut with κ = 3.6, all ε, v ≤ 0.2.
* **Redesigns considered.** (i) Sweeping both arms out to r_0 before the main walk, or a zigzag at geometric scales:
  every turning point leaves an arm behind at a wedge angle (here 2 arcsin v, then O(v) per turn), and by Claim 4 each
  becomes a hook at ratio λ_c; the problem is moved, not removed. (ii) Leaving the front perpendicularly gives the
  benign C^{1,v} shape at once (HEURISTIC, §2), which suggests sweeping the two halves outward from the tip with a
  transverse return trip; not analysed. (iii) Simply cutting at α_s (W_cut) already removes the part of the tip that
  is singular at scale ε.
* **v ≲ 1/log log(1/ε) by iterating Claim 5.** Not obtained, and I believe not obtainable without sin²φ: the reference
  can be refreshed, but the chord-arc constant of the refreshed arc is only controlled by exp(v·J_total), and
  J_total ≥ log(1/ε)/(1+v) for every direction at the moment it is seen (D2-1 §2, PROVED). So the iteration reproduces
  v log(1/ε) ≲ 1. With flatness input the picture changes: if arcs ahead of Harry had chord-arc constant 1 + O(v) at all
  scales, the growth rate would be O(v² log(1/ε)), i.e. v ≲ (log(1/ε))^{-1/2} (HEURISTIC).
