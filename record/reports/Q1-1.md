## 3. What now limits v, and what cannot be gained without changing the method

**Binding: (P6), the near-pair excess increment** — 2v[D_I + D_IIs] ≈ 0.93 of the budget 1 − f² at every optimum, and of
this the base-point part of Term II, 2v·2c_6²κ1C_TL C1A τ_β, is ≈ 0.71–0.75. (P7) is binding only because κ1 is squeezed to
its minimum (κ1 − 1 ≈ 0.02 is free to trade); (C2)/(C4)/(C6) fix R but R hardly matters any more (c_sep 0.024 → 0.1: no
gain; the U/R terms use < 5% of their budgets).

**Ceiling of this bookkeeping.** Within the method, each factor of D_IIs_β has a lower bound independent of the data:
c_6 = (1+δ1)/c_A ≥ 2 (the distance bound ρ ≥ c_A w − δ1ε at w ≈ ε; it is optimal in the form max(ρ,ε) ≥ w/c and cannot be
improved at scale λ ≈ ε, where the whole near period has g ≲ K_fε); C_A ≥ min_q q^{1/2+γ}/(1−q^{−γ}) ≈ 6.75 at γ = 1/2
(≈ 5.4 as γ → 1) — the dyadic tilt chain; C_TL ≥ 2C_A + 3^{1/2+γ}; C1A ≥ C_A; τ_β ≥ 2^γ(2 + 3/4·κ1) (one leg at g ≈ 0
plus the gap integral); K_f > (2δ1+κ1)/c_A + 2 ≈ 5 (c_ρK_f ≥ 2). Hence D ≳ 5·10³ and **v ≲ 1e-4 for every choice of the
parameters and every data set**: the present 5.8–6.5e-5 is within a factor ≈ 1.6 of that ceiling (the rest is the f², D_I,
Term-II-α shares). The remaining slack, all requiring new arguments (= a change of the method's estimates, not of bookkeeping):
(i) estimating the base-point offset p_a by one-step tilts for g ≲ λ instead of the full chain C_TL (≈ 30% of D_IIs_β);
(ii) a distance bound sharper than c_6 at scales ≈ ε; (iii) avoiding the triangle inequality |e_{a′a}×e_ab| ≤ |Δe| + |Δe|.
None of these can move v by more than a factor ≈ 2–3. Getting to, e.g., 1e-3 would need a different near-pair estimate.
