# F-1: trustworthy numerics for the eps > 0 sweep, pushing to v >= 0.8

**Formulation change (Rule 5):** none to the problem itself. All sweep runs use vision radius
eps > 0 (eps/d in {0.1, 0.05, 0.025}); the eps = 0 column uses the discrete chase of Section 4 of
`snitch_proved.tex`. Units d = 1, Harry speed 1. Everything in this report is NUMERICAL unless
explicitly labelled otherwise; nothing here is PROVED.

## Question given
Build a simulator that provably tracks the wavefront Gamma (exact straight steps via the (C,h)
translation, exact-replay resampling with error control), implement greedy / standoff / other
controllers, reproduce the Section 8 table with convergence in every numerical parameter, then push
to v = 0.8, 0.9, 0.95 for eps in {0.1, 0.05}; if the sweep fails, characterise how; fit T(v).

## Result in one paragraph
NUMERICAL throughout. (1) The exact-map, exact-replay simulator reproduces every entry of the published
eps > 0 table to its last digit, with convergence in step size and gap tolerance (Section 2); the eps = 0
column is the N -> infinity limit of the discrete chase, not N = 2000 (Section 3). (2) The v = 0.8 failure
of the brief is explained: the greedy controller has a Hopf-type instability at v* ~ 0.78 (linear model:
1 + a e^w w^{-a} Gamma(a,w) = 0, a = 1/(1-v); confirmed independently of stream H), producing a zigzag whose
amplitude and period scale with eps and do not depend on the step; the zigzag makes the computed
wavefront near Harry exponentially sensitive (30-digit cross-check: error x e^{1.65 t}), and double
precision is exhausted at t ~ 18 (eps = 0.1). (3) A linearly stable "two-point" controller (aim at the
bearing of B extrapolated away from the front's continuation) removes the zigzag at v = 0.8 and sweeps
about half the alpha-circle (alpha_B = 3.0 at t = 142-144, converged to ~1% in dt and tol), but the
unseen length stays ~87 rather than shrinking, and at t ~ 150 (eps = 0.1) Harry runs head-on into the
remaining arm, the front splits into a fast-growing tongue and the computation cannot continue; at eps =
0.05 round-off swamps the front by t ~ 80. **So v = 0.8 is neither shown to be capture nor failure.**
(4) For v >= 0.85 no rotation-invariant two-point controller is linearly stable (one exception at 0.85
that fails nonlinearly) and all nonlinear runs lose control within ~20-40 time units; the open-loop
sensitivity of the front, ~ (R/rho)^{v/(1-v)}, makes double-precision verification of long sweeps
implausible there. (5) Over v = 0.3-0.75, T(v) grows faster than any fixed power of 1/(1-v)
(local exponent 2.7 -> 3.8) and is fit well by T ~ 4.9 (1-v)^{-1.8} e^{0.49/(1-v)} (eps = 0.1).

## 1. Numerical method (code in `code/F/`)

**Kernel (`snitch_core.py: step_map`).** Over a straight unit-speed Harry step of duration dt every
candidate is advanced with the exact (C,h) translation of Prop. 1, written in s = ln tan(beta/2):
cos beta = -tanh s, sin beta = sech s, g = e^{-vs}/cosh s, and

  k(s) = h/C = 1/2 [ (1+v) e^{-(1-v)s} - (1-v) e^{(1+v)s} ]  (strictly decreasing),

so beta' = k^{-1}(h'/C) is found by safeguarded Newton on log k (separate branches k>0, k<0, using
expm1 to avoid cancellation near k = 0, i.e. beta near theta*), with an always-valid bracket. Along a
straight step |beta| increases monotonically and rho' = v - cos beta, so the minimum of rho over the
step is attained at s = -atanh v (cos beta = v) if that is crossed, else at an endpoint; the exact
time at which rho first equals eps is obtained by a monotone 1-D root find in s and converted to time
through h. "Seen" is therefore decided exactly (up to round-off) for every candidate.
Degenerate cases (beta = 0 or pi exactly) are handled separately.

Verification (`test_kernel.py`): 400 random/adversarial steps (beta down to 1e-8 and up to pi - 1e-8,
v up to 0.95) agree with DOP853 at rtol 1e-13 to max relative error 4.4e-14; seen-times agree with an
event-located DOP853 solve to 8e-15; k^{-1} round-trips to 6e-15 for |s| <= 300, v up to 0.99.

**Wavefront representation (`sim.py`).** Gamma is a circularly ordered list of candidates labelled by
their initial bearing alpha = kc*pi/2 + off with |off| <= pi/4 (four charts, so the label and the
azimuthal coordinate of the initial point keep full relative precision near alpha = 0, pi/2, pi, 3pi/2;
the first version used a signed alpha and lost precision at alpha ~ pi, see Section 10). After each Harry step, every neighbouring pair with at least one unseen member whose
current Euclidean gap exceeds tol = max(tau*eps, eta*min(rho_i, rho_j)) (eta = tau unless stated)
gets a midpoint label; the new candidate is integrated **from t = 0 along the recorded piecewise
straight Harry path with the same kernel** (bitwise identical to forward advancement), including its
exact seen status and seen time. This repeats until no gap exceeds tol. Seen candidates whose two
neighbours are both seen are dropped; an arc between two adjacent seen candidates is declared seen
(this is correct up to an effective vision radius eps + O(tol), because the gap was <= tol while
either endpoint was unseen). The sweep ends when no unseen candidate remains; T is the exact seen time
of the last one.

**Re-anchoring (triggered only at v >= 0.8, see Sections 4, 9).** When a midpoint label is within 2^20
ulps of its neighbours, the curve is first refined exactly to tol/4, then the current candidate
polyline becomes the reference curve of a new epoch, labelled by chord length; later insertions are
initialised by cubic Hermite interpolation (Bessel tangents) on that reference and replayed exactly
from the epoch start. Points interpolated in a reference segment with one seen end take the status of
the nearer end. This is *not* exact; the interpolation offset |cubic - linear| is logged.
Forced periodic re-anchoring changes T by <= 0.06% at v = 0.3, 0.5 (eps = 0.1), comparable to the
tau-dependence, so it is not a dominant error where it was tested.

**Controllers.** `greedy`: head for the first unseen candidate B in sweep order (alpha increasing
from 0). `lead`: head for the point at arclength lam*eps beyond B on the unseen polyline.
`standoff`: keep distance sigma*eps from the local candidate polyline, heading = v n + sqrt(1-v^2) t
plus proportional correction. `mpc`: receding-horizon search: for each of a fan of headings, run
straight for hfac*eps (exact map), score the contiguous arclength of front, starting at B, that gets
seen (with partial credit for the next point), take the best heading for one step.

**Discretisation parameters:** Harry step dt = dtf*eps; gap tolerance tau*eps near Harry and
eta*rho far away. Every number below is given with its (dtf, tau).

## 4. Why the greedy sweep "loses the wavefront" at v = 0.8 - NUMERICAL (mechanism) + CONJECTURE (interpretation)

Two distinct effects, the first causing the second.

**(a) Greedy is dynamically unstable above v ~ 0.78; the instability is a property of the
continuous-time controller, not of the step size, and it is scale-invariant in eps.**
* Path plots (`python explore_path.py out/paths_early.png 0.7,0.8 0.1,0.05 17 0.1`,
  `python explore_path.py out/paths_bif.png 0.72,0.74,0.76,0.78 0.1 15 0.1`): for v <= 0.74 the heading
  oscillation after the first turn decays; at 0.76 a small oscillation persists to t = 15; at 0.78 and 0.8
  Harry's path is a regular "staircase"/zigzag. Mean |turn rate| over the second half: 0.15 (v=0.7),
  0.19 (0.72), 0.29 (0.74), 0.62 (0.76), 2.0 (0.78), 2.8-4.5 (0.8). Halving dt (dtf 0.1 -> 0.05) leaves the
  v = 0.8 zigzag unchanged (2.76 vs 2.89).
* Halving eps halves the zigzag's amplitude and period (`out/paths_v08_eps.png`): measured heading period
  13.8 eps (eps = 0.1) and 15.0 eps (eps = 0.05) (`python greedy_period.py`). This is what the local scale
  invariance predicts: near Harry, on scales << the curvature radius of Gamma, the only length is eps.
* Linear theory (independently of stream H; `lin_stab.py`, `lin_count.py`): a front point at
  time-to-arrival sigma (distance (1-v) sigma ahead) with lateral offset y obeys, to first order,
  y' = mu y/sigma - theta, mu = v/(1-v), so y sigma^mu = Y - int (t_a - s)^mu theta(s) ds (the Abel
  equation reported by H). Greedy is theta = bearing of the point at distance eps. Modes theta ~ e^{lambda t}
  give, with w = lambda eps/(1-v) and a = 1/(1-v),

      1 + a e^w w^{-a} Gamma(a, w) = 0.

  Argument-principle counts (robust, no root guesses) and Newton roots: no right-half-plane zero for
  v <= 0.76; a conjugate pair crosses at v* = 0.780 (w = 3e-5 + 2.85i at v = 0.78); w = 0.240 + 3.128i at
  v = 0.8 (growth 0.048/eps, linear period 10.0 eps); 2 pairs at 0.9, 3 at 0.95. This agrees with H's v* = 0.780.
  The observed nonlinear limit cycle has period ~14 eps (longer than the linear 10 eps, as expected at
  finite amplitude).

**(b) The zigzag makes the computed wavefront near Harry exponentially sensitive, and double precision
fails at t ~ 18 (eps = 0.1) / t ~ 11 (eps = 0.05).**
* High-precision check (`python mp_check.py 0.8 0.1 T1 24 30 greedy`): the 24 unseen candidates nearest
  Harry are re-integrated along the *same recorded path* with the same exact map in 30-digit mpmath.
  Max |double - mp| = 2e-12 (T1 = 5), 6e-8 (T1 = 10), 2.3e-4 (T1 = 15), i.e. growth ~ e^{1.65 t}; it
  reaches the gap tolerance 0.01 at t ~ 17-18.
* Exactly then the simulator sees: midpoint labels alpha no longer representable (first `prec_fail` at
  t = 18.21), local stretch |dS/dalpha| ~ 7e11 at alpha ~ 1.35e-4, and the candidate count explodes from
  ~500 to >20000 within 0.1 time units as round-off noise is resolved as "wavefront". This is the
  failure reported in the brief.
* Re-anchoring (Section 1) cures the label precision but not the round-off amplification: the greedy
  re-anchored runs at v = 0.8 also stall at t ~ 18 / 11.

Interpretation (CONJECTURE): the v = 0.8 failure of the brief is the greedy controller's Hopf-type
instability (a), plus the resulting loss of significance (b). It says nothing about whether capture is
possible at v = 0.8. Note the instability rate scales like 1/eps, so for fixed v > 0.78 greedy gets
*worse* as eps -> 0.

## 5. Linearly stable controllers (linear model) - NUMERICAL (root counts) / heuristic model

Linear model (Section 4a): with weights g_j summing to 1 (rotation invariance), the controller
theta = sum_j g_j psi(c_j eps) (psi(r) = bearing of the front point at distance r) has characteristic
function

    f(w) = 1 + sum_j g_j a L_a(c_j w),   L_a(z) = int_0^inf (1+x)^{a-1} e^{-zx} dx = e^z z^{-a} Gamma(a,z),

w = lambda eps/(1-v), a = 1/(1-v). Zeros in Re w > 0 are counted by the argument principle on
[1e-4, 80] x [-80, 80] (log-spaced near the branch point at 0; cross-checked against Newton roots for
greedy). Code: `lin_count.py`, `lin_multi.py`.

* Pure pursuit of a single point at any distance c*eps is exactly as unstable as greedy (only the time
  scale changes) - there is no length scale in the linear model.
* PD/phase lead theta = psi_B + kd sigma_B psi_B' : stable at v = 0.8 only for kd ~ 1; unstable for all
  tested kd at v >= 0.85. In the full simulation the derivative of psi_B is too noisy (B jumps as points
  are seen): PD made the zigzag worse (mean turn rate 6.5-10.6 vs 2.8).
* **Two-point "extrapolating" controller** theta = psi_B + k (psi_C - psi_B), C the front point at
  distance c*eps, k < 0 (i.e. aim on the far side of B, away from the front's continuation):
  v = 0.8: stable for k <= -1 (c = 1.5..10) and for k = -0.5 when c >= 2; unstable for all k >= -0.25.
  v = 0.85: stable only at (k, c) = (-5, 1.5) among k in {-5,-3,-2,-1,-0.5}, c in {1.5,2,3,5,10};
  v = 0.9, 0.95: unstable for every tested (k, c).
* Interpretation (heuristic): the open-loop kernel a(1+x)^{a-1} gives a phase lag ~ a*pi/2 at low
  frequency; a rotation-invariant combination of look-ahead bearings can only cancel it by cancelling
  ~a moments of the kernel, i.e. by an order-~a finite difference of the front profile at scale eps -
  the "derivative of order 1/(1-v)" of the brief. Weights grow combinatorially with a.

    candidates nearest Harry: below the gap tolerance 0.01 but no longer negligible.
  Interpretation (CONJECTURE): running head-on into a front that lies along the forward line splits it;
  material on either side of the line is thrown sideways with amplification (R/rho)^{1/(1-v)} = (R/rho)^5,
  creating a rapidly lengthening tongue that must also be swept. Whether the true (exact) front does
  this, and whether the sweep could still finish, is not decided by these runs; the subsequent growth
  rate of n (~18000 candidates per unit time at tol 0.01) is far above what the length bound
  L' <= v * (bearing variation) allows for a smooth tongue near Harry, so the post-onset curve is
  probably partly resolved noise.
* eps = 0.05: the run stalled (candidate cascade) at t ~ 82, alpha_B = 1.64, i.e. *before* the
  front-splitting point. Here the cause is conditioning: the 30-digit cross-check gives max error
  1.1e-4 at t = 40 and 4.5e-3 at t = 70 against a gap tolerance of 5e-3 (`python mp_check.py 0.8 0.05 40,70,80 12 30 two`),
  so by t ~ 80 the computed front near Harry is round-off dominated. Halving eps made the error ~100x
  larger at comparable times (eps = 0.1: 5.6e-5 at t = 60).

**Status for v = 0.8: neither capture nor failure is established.** A linearly stable controller sweeps
about half of the alpha-circle in a reproducible, converged way, with a non-shrinking unseen length,
and then hits a front-splitting event that the present double-precision method cannot follow.

## 9. v = 0.85, 0.9, 0.95 - NUMERICAL (failure characterisation only)

* Linear model: greedy has 2 / 4 / 6 right-half-plane zeros at v = 0.85 / 0.9 / 0.95 (growth rates
  1.2, 3.4, 10.8 in units of (1-v)/eps, i.e. very fast and increasing as eps -> 0). All tested
  rotation-invariant two-point controllers are unstable at 0.9 and 0.95; at 0.85 one (k = -5, c = 1.5)
  is linearly stable, and a moment-cancelling four-point combination (J = 3, `lin_multi.py`) is linearly
  stable for h = 0.5, 1, 2. Moment cancellation with J ~ a points (the "order-a derivative") produced
  weights up to 1e16 and was unstable - not usable.
* Nonlinear runs to t = 40 (`python explore_pd.py V EPS 40 0 1` for greedy, `... -1 2 two`):

* What the failures look like: the heading oscillates (turn rates 3-11 versus 0.2 for a stable sweep),

## 10. What was tried and failed, and why
* Exact replay from t = 0 with double-precision bearing labels alpha: fails at v = 0.8 once the local
  stretch |dS/dalpha| exceeds ~1e13 (greedy, t ~ 18) and, with signed labels, at alpha ~ pi. Fixed by
  four-chart labels (full relative precision near alpha = 0, pi/2, pi, 3pi/2) and by re-anchoring.
* Re-anchoring (interpolated new reference curve): harmless where tested (<= 0.06% change in T at
  v = 0.3, 0.5), but it cannot cure round-off amplification of the front *shape*, which is the real limit.
* Controllers: `lead` (aim lam*eps ahead along the front) and the explicit `standoff` (keep distance
  sigma*eps from the local polyline) were worse than greedy at v = 0.8 (standoff as implemented picked the
  wrong local branch at the start and circled); receding-horizon `mpc` (exact straight-run look-ahead,
  hfac = 1, 3, 6) reproduced greedy's zigzag; phase-lead `pd` made it worse (noisy derivative of the
  bearing of B, which jumps as points are seen). Low-pass "heading smoothing" adds lag and cannot help
  (linear theory: phase lag ~ a*pi/2 is the problem).
* Pure pursuit of a single look-ahead point at any distance: identical stability to greedy (scale
  invariance of the linear model).
* Parallel replay with numba threads under heavy machine load: 40 ms overhead per call; replaced by serial
  replay per process and process-level parallelism.

## 11. Single most useful next step
Run the v = 0.8 two-point sweep past the front-splitting event in extended precision for the candidates
near Harry (e.g. double-double positions plus the existing exact map, or mpmath for a local window of
~1e3 candidates replayed from a checkpoint), to decide whether the tongue at t ~ 150 is a true geometric
feature that the controller must then sweep (and whether L_unseen ever starts to shrink), or an artefact.
In parallel, design controllers that avoid running head-on along a radially aligned arm (e.g. a
constraint keeping the front at bearing >= beta_0 > 0 ahead), since that is exactly the configuration
with maximal (R/rho)^{v/(1-v)} amplification.
