## 0. Question
Goal 1: an UNCONDITIONAL ε = 0 capture theorem for some explicit range 0 < v < v0. Route to try first: the Abel bound
W ≤ 2 + (turning of the remaining polyline) ≤ 2 + TC(t), TC(t) = total absolute curvature of the true wavefront arc
γ_t = {S_α(t) : α ∈ [α_j, α_N]}; prove TC ≤ K0 uniformly, giving Theorem B for v < 1/(3 + K0). If it fails, say exactly
why (with numbers) and try the best alternative for small v (perturbation around v = 0; bearing-sorted chase).

## 1. Result in one paragraph
**No unconditional ε = 0 theorem was obtained.** The curvature route fails as a proof strategy, for a precise reason,
and numerically it would not even be competitive with the existing conditional hypotheses.
(i) NUMERICAL. Along dyadic chases (N ≤ 4095, v = 0.02–0.2, refinement-converged) sup_t TC(t) = TC(t0) ≈ 2π − 2 arcsin v
(except v = 0.2, N = 4095: 5.92 vs 5.88, the hook below starting to show)
and TC then decreases roughly in proportion to the remaining arc; but in the N → ∞ limit (deep-tail probes) TC jumps
**during the first leg** by a "hook" at the tail end of the arc, to sup TC ≈ 6.97 (v = 0.2), 7.39 (0.1), 7.58 (0.05),
≥ 7.66 (0.02) — i.e. **above 2π**; the jump is entirely in the tail and is produced in time O(ρ_N(t0)) → 0.
Small within-leg increases (+8·10⁻³ per leg at v = 0.2) also occur at the end of every leg (boundary layer at the target).
The number of inflection points stayed ≤ 2 in every resolved run. W ≤ 2.15 throughout, far below 2 + TC.
(ii) PROVED. The Abel–Fáry bound in sharp form (Lemma A3.1); the exact pointwise evolution laws of the tangent angle and
curvature of the wavefront (τ_t = (v/2ρ) sin 2φ; Lemma A3.2) and the resulting rate formula TC' = Σ ±2Ω at the inflection
points ± endpoint terms (Prop. A3.3). These show why the route fails: the rate is ~ v/ρ at inflection points and
endpoints near Harry, which is unbounded uniformly in N (the hook forms at distance ρ_N(t0) → 0 from Harry, and the target
end has distance → 0 at every catch), and the inflection equation has no comparison principle (it is an ODE in t for each α,
with a source term), so the inflection count is not monotone a priori. A uniform TC bound is equivalent in difficulty to
the near-field stability of the discrete chase (A-2 §5, B-1/H-1 zigzag), which nobody can prove yet.
(iii) PROVED, new and of independent use: **hull-perimeter lemma** (Lemma A3.4): for ANY chase in which Harry heads at a
remaining candidate, P = perimeter of conv({H} ∪ remaining) never jumps and P' ≤ −(1 + cos B_H) + v(π + B_H) a.e.,
where B_H is the opening of the smallest sector at Harry containing the remaining candidates (no ordering, no W, no A).
Consequently (Theorem A3.5, PROVED conditional) Theorem B/B2 hold with the end-phase hypothesis replaced by the sector
condition **(E_H)**: after t_f the remaining candidates lie in a sector at H of opening ≤ γ, with 1 + cos γ > v(π + γ).
The admissible opening γ_H(v) → π as v → 0 (π − γ_H ≈ 2√(πv)), whereas A-2's cone condition needs γ < arccos v → π/2
(and A-2 found the measured cone approaching π/2 as v → 0). Measured sup B_H after t_f (deep tail): 0.92, 1.02, 0.95, 0.84,
0.75, 0.65, 0.56, 0.52 for v = 0.1, 0.2, 0.24, 0.3, 0.35, 0.4, 0.45, 0.47 against γ_H = 2.07, 1.62, 1.47, 1.25, 1.07, 0.90,
0.73, 0.66. So (E_H) is the robust end-phase hypothesis at small v; it is still a hypothesis.
(iv) Alternatives (perturbation in v, bearing-sorted chase, hull perimeter alone): each fails at the same point — all
controllable quantities are coarse (positions are within vt of the static ones), while W, the bearing order and even B_H
depend on the configuration within distance ≈ vT of Harry, which contains ≈ N·vT/2π candidates (§6).

## 2. Numerical method (NUMERICAL)
`code/A3/flow.py`. Each straight run is the exact map of Prop. 1 ((C, h) ↦ (C, h − (1−v²)s), bearing recovered by
bisection + Newton). **Tangent vectors** T = ∂_αS are transported exactly with the invariants of the run:
dlogC = [v sin b T_x + (1 − v cos b)T_y]/y and dh = (1 + v cos b)T_x + v sin b T_y (heading frame, left point, y = ρ sin b);
at the new point T is recovered from the 2×2 system [[v sin b, 1 − v cos b], [1 + v cos b, v sin b]] T = [y dlogC, dh]
whose determinant is v² − 1 (well conditioned for all v < 1). Points on the forward/backward ray: T_y scales by
(ρ0/ρ)^{v/(1−v)} resp. (ρ/ρ0)^{v/(1+v)}. Positions and tangents at t0 are computed stably from h(t0) = −2 sin²(a/2),
C = g(a), dlogC/da = (cos a − v)/sin a, dh/da = −sin a, with the tail given by its exact complement a = 2π − α (needed for
clusters down to a ≈ 10⁻¹⁹⁴).
Validation (`test_flow.py`): against DOP853 (rtol 1e−12) for the position + variational equation: initial run max error
2·10⁻¹⁴ (positions), 1.7·10⁻¹³ (unit tangents); 90 random runs (v = 0.1, 0.3, 0.45): 4.8·10⁻¹⁴, 1.7·10⁻¹³. Capture times
reproduce A-1: 13.1948 (v = 0.2) and 15.1502 (v = 0.24) at N = 1023.
TC (`tc.py`): the chase is run on the candidates and its legs recorded; then a grid refining every gap m-fold
(α = α_i + (α_{i+1} − α_i)l/m) is pushed through the recorded path; TC(t) ≈ Σ |wrap(τ_{k+1} − τ_k)| over the samples of
[α_j, α_N] (τ = tangent angle). This is a lower bound for the true TC converging as m grows; convergence was checked by
doubling m and by the Fáry inequality (polyline turning ≤ TC must hold; a violation flags under-resolution).
Snapshots: every leg start and fractions 0.5, 0.9, 0.99 (or 0.1, 0.25, …) of every leg.

## 4. Proved results

### Lemma A3.1 (Abel–Fáry bound, sharp form). PROVED
During leg j, at a time when no remaining candidate is at H, let e_i = (S_{i+1} − S_i)/|S_{i+1} − S_i| (j ≤ i ≤ N − 1),
κ_i ∈ [0, π] the turning angle of the polyline at S_i (j < i < N), and when κ_i < π let ψ_i be the angle between r̂_i and
the bisector e_{i−1} + e_i. Then
  W = r̂_N·e_{N−1} − r̂_j·e_j + Σ_{i=j+1}^{N−1} r̂_i·(e_{i−1} − e_i),  |r̂_i·(e_{i−1} − e_i)| = 2 sin(κ_i/2)|sin ψ_i|,
hence W ≤ r̂_N·e_{N−1} − u·e_j + Σ 2 sin(κ_i/2)|sin ψ_i| ≤ 2 + Σ κ_i. If moreover no direction in [α_j, α_N] has been caught
by time t (so γ_t is a continuous injective arc), then Σ κ_i ≤ TC(γ_t), where TC is the total curvature in the sense of
Milnor/Sullivan (sup of the turning of inscribed polygons; = ∫|κ| ds + Σ corner angles for piecewise C² arcs).
So **W ≤ 2 + TC(γ_t)**.
*Proof.* Summation by parts of W = Σ_{i=j}^{N−1}(r̂_{i+1} − r̂_i)·e_i. Since |e_{i−1}| = |e_i| = 1, e_{i−1} − e_i is orthogonal
to e_{i−1} + e_i and has length 2 sin(κ_i/2); so its scalar product with r̂_i is 2 sin(κ_i/2) times the component of r̂_i
orthogonal to the bisector (if κ_i = π the bound 2 sin(κ_i/2) = 2 still holds). 2 sin(x/2) ≤ x. The candidate polyline is
inscribed in γ_t with vertices in curve order (the chain order is the α order). Refinement never decreases turning:
inserting X between consecutive vertices A, B, the direction of AB is a positive combination of those of AX and XB, so it
lies on the shorter arc between them and ∠(AX, AB) + ∠(AB, XB) = ∠(AX, XB); two triangle inequalities on the circle of
directions give ∠(e_PA, e_AB) + ∠(e_AB, e_BQ) ≤ ∠(e_PA, e_AX) + ∠(e_AX, e_XB) + ∠(e_XB, e_BQ). Hence the turning of the
candidate polyline is ≤ the sup over inscribed polygons, which is TC (Milnor 1950; J. M. Sullivan, *Curves of finite
total curvature*, 2008, for the equality with ∫|κ|ds + corners). ∎
*Remark.* The sharp form shows that the turning only costs where the chain is non-radial (|sin ψ_i| large); in the
near-radial end phase the endpoint terms almost cancel (r̂_N·e_{N−1} ≈ 1 ≈ u·e_j). The continuum analogue is
W_c := ∫ sin²φ/ρ ds = [cos φ]_{ends} − ∫ κ (r̂·N) ds (φ = angle between tangent and r̂), and L_arc' = v W_c exactly.
Numerically max(W − 2 − TC) ≈ −2.0 in every run: the bound is very lossy.

### Lemma A3.2 (evolution of the wavefront's tangent angle and curvature). PROVED
Let H be any path with |H'| ≤ 1 and let I be an interval of directions none of which is caught on [t1, t2]. Write
T = ∂_αS, τ = its angle, θ = the polar angle of r = S − H, φ = τ − θ, κ = ∂_ατ/|T| (signed curvature), and
Ω := (v/2ρ) sin 2φ. Then T never vanishes, and on I × [t1, t2]
  ∂_t τ = Ω = (v/2ρ) sin 2φ,   ∂_t log|T| = (v/ρ) sin²φ,
  ∂_t κ = (v/ρ)(cos 2φ − sin²φ) κ − (v/ρ²) sin φ (3cos²φ − 1),
  ∂_t(∂_ατ) = (v/ρ) cos 2φ ∂_ατ − (v|T|/ρ²) sin φ (3cos²φ − 1).
None of these contains H'; each direction evolves by an ODE in t alone (no α-derivatives of τ on the right of the first law).
*Proof.* Differentiating (1) in α: ∂_tT = (v/ρ)(I − r̂r̂ᵀ)T (paper, Prop. 6). T(t) = M(t)T(t1) with M the fundamental matrix
of a linear ODE with continuous coefficients, so T ≠ 0. With r̂⊥ the rotation of r̂ by +90°, T = |T|(cos φ r̂ + sin φ r̂⊥) and
(I − r̂r̂ᵀ)T = |T| sin φ r̂⊥; projecting on T̂ and T̂⊥ (r̂⊥·T̂ = sin φ, r̂⊥·T̂⊥ = cos φ) gives ∂_t|T| = (v/ρ) sin²φ |T| and
|T|∂_tτ = (v/ρ)|T| sin φ cos φ. Differentiate ∂_tτ in α using ∂_αρ = |T| cos φ, ∂_αθ = |T| sin φ/ρ, ∂_αφ = ∂_ατ − ∂_αθ:
∂_α Ω = −(v/2ρ²)|T| cos φ sin 2φ + (v/ρ) cos 2φ (∂_ατ − |T| sin φ/ρ) = (v/ρ) cos 2φ ∂_ατ − (v|T|/ρ²) sin φ (cos²φ + cos 2φ),
and cos²φ + cos 2φ = 3cos²φ − 1. Finally ∂_tκ = ∂_t(∂_ατ)/|T| − κ ∂_t log|T|. ∎
Checks: circle about a fixed Harry (φ = π/2, κ = 1/R): κ_t = −v/R² = d(1/R)/dt with R' = v. Radial line: κ_t = 0.
Finite-difference test on random arcs (`test_laws.py`): relative errors 4.6·10⁻⁷ (τ_t), 1.1·10⁻⁴ (κ_t, O(h²) differencing).

### Proposition A3.3 (rate of the total curvature). PROVED
On a time interval inside one leg, suppose τ(·, t) is C² on [α_j, α_N] with finitely many interior critical points
c_1(t) < … < c_m(t), each a simple zero of ∂_ατ depending C¹ on t (c_0 = α_j, c_{m+1} = α_N). Then
TC(t) = Σ_{k=0}^{m} |τ(c_{k+1}) − τ(c_k)| and
  TC'(t) = Σ_{k=0}^{m} σ_k [Ω(c_{k+1}) − Ω(c_k)],  σ_k = sign of ∂_ατ on (c_k, c_{k+1}),
i.e. +2Ω at every local maximum of τ, −2Ω at every local minimum, ±Ω at the two ends. In particular
|TC'| ≤ (v/2)[1/ρ(α_j) + 1/ρ(α_N) + 2 Σ_k 1/ρ(c_k)].
*Proof.* On each (c_k, c_{k+1}) τ is strictly monotone, so the variation there is |τ(c_{k+1}) − τ(c_k)|. Differentiate:
d/dt τ(c_k(t), t) = ∂_ατ·c_k' + ∂_tτ = Ω(c_k) because ∂_ατ(c_k) = 0; the ends are fixed within a leg. ∎
*Remark (no comparison principle).* By Lemma A3.2, ∂_ατ at a fixed α obeys a linear ODE in t with source
F = −(v|T|/ρ²) sin φ(3cos²φ − 1). At a point where ∂_ατ and ∂²_ατ vanish (and ∂³_ατ = c ≠ 0), ∂_ατ ≈ c(α − a)²/2 + F(t − t*),
so a pair of inflections is created on the side of t* where F(t − t*)/c < 0. F vanishes only where the tangent is radial
(sin φ = 0) or at |cos φ| = 1/√3, so nothing local forbids creation: there is no Sturm/maximum principle for the inflection
count (unlike curve-shortening flows, there is no diffusion in α). That the count stayed ≤ 2 numerically is a global fact.

## 5. Why the curvature route fails (exactly)
1. *The rate is not uniformly bounded.* By Prop. A3.3 every inflection point and both ends contribute ~v/ρ. Two of them
   come arbitrarily close to Harry, uniformly in nothing: (a) the tail end at t0, at distance ρ_N(t0) ≈ c·(2π/M)^{(1+v)/(1−v)}
   (→ 0 as N → ∞): it creates the hook of §3.2, a jump of TC by ≈ 1.0–1.5 within time O(ρ_N(t0)); (b) the target end, whose
   distance → 0 at every catch: it creates the boundary layer (+8·10⁻³ per leg at v = 0.2), which is cut off by the catch.
   Any estimate of the form TC' ≤ C·v, or TC' ≤ C·v·TC, is therefore false uniformly in N.
2. *Bounded-rotation arguments do not close either.* Each tangent can rotate by at most π/2 towards φ = ±π/2 before
   stalling (Ω = 0 there), so (a) and (b) produce O(1) curvature each. But the part of the boundary layer deposited on
   S_{j+1}, S_{j+2}, … (distance ~ one gap from Harry when the leg starts) is not cut off; whether these O(v) deposits
   stay coherent (curvature O(v), as the numerics show) or accumulate leg after leg (a zigzag, TV ~ v·N) is exactly the
   linear stability of the discrete chase (B-1 B5, H-1 H3: stable for v < 1/2 in the linear model), now needed
   *nonlinearly and uniformly in N*. TC is not easier than W.
3. *TC is not even ≤ 2π in the limit* (7.0–7.7, §3.2), and with the best numerical K0 ≈ 8 the bound W ≤ 2 + TC would cover
   only v < 1/(3 + K0) ≈ 0.09, whereas A-2's conditional (E_K) covers v < v1 = 0.4748 with W ≤ 0.82 observed.
4. The Sturm idea fails structurally (Remark after Prop. A3.3).

## 6. Alternatives for small v

### Lemma A3.4 (hull perimeter). PROVED
Let R(t) be the set of remaining candidates of any chase in which, on each leg, Harry moves with constant velocity u,
|u| = 1, pointing at a remaining candidate (chain chase, bearing-sorted chase, …), and let
P(t) = perimeter of K(t) = conv({H(t)} ∪ R(t)) (P = 2·length for a segment). Let B_H(t) ∈ [0, π] be the opening of the
smallest closed sector with apex H containing R(t), B_H := π if no sector of opening < π contains it. Then:
(a) P is continuous on [t0, T_end] (in particular it does not jump at planned or accidental catches) and Lipschitz;
(b) for a.e. t, P'(t) ≤ −(1 + cos B_H) + v(π + B_H) (≤ 2πv always);
(c) P ≤ 2Φ, and P ≥ 2 max_i ρ_i, so P = 0 iff the chase is over.
*Proof.* (a) A candidate is removed only at the instant it coincides with H, which leaves K unchanged; between removals
every point of {H} ∪ R moves with speed ≤ 1, and the perimeter of the hull of finitely many points is Lipschitz in them
(Cauchy's formula below). (b) Cauchy: P = ∫_0^{2π} h(θ, t) dθ with support function h = max over p ∈ {H} ∪ R of ⟨p, e_θ⟩.
For each θ, h(θ, ·) is a max of finitely many C¹ functions (candidates are C¹ while ρ > 0; H is affine on a leg), so it has
a right derivative D⁺h(θ, t) = max over active p of ⟨p', e_θ⟩ (Danskin), and the difference quotients are bounded by 1;
by dominated convergence P'⁺(t) = ∫ D⁺h(θ, t) dθ. Except for finitely many θ (normals of hull edges) the active point is
unique and is the vertex V whose normal arc N_V contains θ. Hence P'⁺ = Σ_V ⟨V', ∫_{N_V} e_θ dθ⟩ = Σ_V 2 sin(ε_V/2)⟨V', n_V⟩,
where ε_V = |N_V| is the exterior angle and n_V the outer bisector; Σ_V ε_V = 2π. A candidate vertex has V' = v r̂_V, so it
contributes ≤ 2v sin(ε_V/2) ≤ vε_V. If H is a vertex, its interior angle is B_H < π, ε_H = π − B_H, and u (pointing at a
point of R) lies in the interior sector, at angle x ≤ B_H/2 from −n_H; so H contributes
2 sin((π − B_H)/2)(−cos x) ≤ −2cos²(B_H/2) = −(1 + cos B_H). If H is not a vertex it contributes 0 = −(1 + cos π) and
ε_H := 0 ≤ π − B_H. Summing: P'⁺ ≤ −(1 + cos B_H) + v(2π − ε_H) ≤ −(1 + cos B_H) + v(π + B_H). Where P is differentiable
(a.e.) P' = P'⁺. (c) K = conv of the polyline H S_j … S_N of length Φ; by Cauchy, P = ∫_0^π w(θ)dθ with w the width, and
w(θ) ≤ Σ_edges |⟨edge, e_θ⟩|, ∫_0^π |⟨e, e_θ⟩| dθ = 2|e|. P ≥ 2 diam K ≥ 2 max ρ_i. ∎
Numerical test (`hull_check.py`): 150 random sets (v ∈ [0.02, 0.7], 2–30 directions, 30 % with a near-twin pair at gap
10⁻⁹…10⁻³), 100 860 configurations, exact map: max[ΔP − ∫bound]/Δt = −0.025 ≤ 0; max(P − 2Φ) = 2·10⁻¹³; P continuous at
catches to O(10⁻⁷τ) (the residual of stopping 10⁻⁷τ before the catch). (A first version evaluated the bound at the catch
instant itself, where the target's direction is rounding noise, and reported a spurious 0.71; `hull_debug.py`.)

### Theorem A3.5 (capture conditional on an end-phase sector condition). PROVED (conditional)
Let D_1 ⊂ D_2 ⊂ … be finite direction sets containing 0 with dense union (e.g. dyadic) and v < v1 = 0.4747526383.
Suppose there is γ ∈ [0, π] with c_H := 1 + cos γ − v(π + γ) > 0 such that
  **(E_H, γ)**: for the chain chase of every D_k, at every t ∈ [t_f, T_end), the remaining candidates lie in a closed
  sector with apex H(t) and opening ≤ γ (t_f = first loss of bearing order, A-1).
Then there is a path with |H'| ≤ 1 catching every direction exactly by
  T = d/(1−v) + 2πd/((1−v)μ),  μ = sup_{0≤λ<1} min{ m(v, λ), (1 − λ)c_H/2 } > 0,
m(v, λ) = min_{0≤x≤π}[(1−λ)(1−v) + λ(1−cos x) − vx] (stream B). For v < 1/(1+π), λ = 0 gives μ ≥ min{1 − v(1+π), c_H/2}.
*Proof.* Ordered phase [t0, t_f]: exactly as in A-2 Thm A2.3: Ψ = Φ − λρ_{N(t)} has Ψ' ≤ −m(v, λ) (A-1 Lemma A3(a): A = β_N,
W ≤ A; B-1 Lemma B1), only downward jumps, Ψ(t0) ≤ Φ(t0) ≤ 2πd/(1−v). End phase: by Lemma A3.4(b) and (E_H, γ),
P' ≤ −(1 + cos B_H) + v(π + B_H) ≤ −c_H (the right side is increasing in B on [0, π], its derivative being
sin B + v > 0, so under B_H ≤ γ it is at most its value at γ, which is −c_H). P is continuous and
P > 0 before T_end, so T_end − t_f ≤ P(t_f)/c_H ≤ 2Φ(t_f)/c_H ≤ 2Ψ(t_f)/((1 − λ)c_H). Adding,
T_end − t0 ≤ (Ψ(t0) − Ψ(t_f))/m + 2Ψ(t_f)/((1−λ)c_H) ≤ Ψ(t0)/min{m, (1−λ)c_H/2}. Every H_k is 1-Lipschitz on [0, T] (it
stays put after its last catch); apply Lemma 4 of the paper. ∎

### 6.1 Why neither the hull perimeter nor perturbation in v gives an unconditional result
* *Hull alone.* At t0 the remaining set subtends 2 arccos v at H (A-1 Lemma A1), so 1 + cos B_H ≈ 2v² ≪ 2πv: P increases
  at first, and stays non-decreasing as long as the tail is behind Harry (static picture: B_H = θ/2, θ = remaining arc,
  so P decreases only once θ ≤ 2π − c√v). The early phase must be covered by Φ (fine while ordered) — so the hull only
  replaces the end-phase hypothesis, it does not remove it.
* *Perturbation in v.* What is controlled for small v is coarse: |S_α(t) − S_α(0)| ≤ vt, pairwise distances
  non-decreasing (E1), Harry within 2vT + gap of the static point e^{iα_j} during leg j. From this one gets (sketch, not
  written out, CONJECTURE-level), for points
  at distance r ≫ vT from Harry, bearings within O(vT/r) of the static ones, and B_H ≤ θ/2 + O(√(vT)) — but only after
  discarding the candidates within distance ~vT of Harry. Those number ≈ N·vT/(2π) → ∞, their directions from Harry are
  not controlled by positions, and they decide the heading, the bearing order, W, and B_H (one of them slightly behind
  Harry makes B_H = π). Replacing H by a disc of radius vT in the hull removes them from B_H but then the disc's motion
  contributes ⟨u, n⟩ with u (the heading towards a near target) uncontrolled: a zigzag at scale vT wastes time without
  decreasing anything. So a small-v proof needs the same near-field statement as everything else: *the chain near
  Harry stays smooth at the gap scale* (turning angles O(gap)), i.e. nonlinear stability of the B-1/H-1 recursion, with
  small coefficients when v is small. Heuristic support (CONJECTURE-level): for a smooth arc through Harry,
  κ_t = −2v h_⊥/ρ³ + O(φ²) by Lemma A3.2 (h_⊥ = Harry's distance from the arc), so the curvature near the target changes by
  a relative O(v) per leg and the far-field deposits are summable (Σ_k (1/k)²); the hook at the tail is the only O(1)
  non-smooth feature and is far from Harry until the end phase.
* *Bearing-sorted chase.* Bearings stay in [0, π] (A-2 §4.4), so B_H = max bearing and Lemma A3.4 applies, but B_H ≈ π
  early (same as above), and Φ_s jumps up by up to 2|PQ| at every tie; no functional was found that pays for the ties
  (P does not jump but does not decrease while B_H ≈ π). Combining G = Φ_s + μP gives G' ≤ −(1 − v(1 + π + 2πμ)) between
  ties, so everything reduces to bounding the total tie cost — a near-field statement again.
